<planner_finding>
**Why the fix is `FocusVisibility(hovered = hovered)` and not a fresh instance — the fold, traced.**

`FocusVisibility` carries three fields. Two are focus-derived (`focused`, and `pointerAcquired`, which is only ever set by a press that arrived while hovered). One, `hovered`, is pointer-derived: its own KDoc says it mirrors the raw interaction stream. `Unfocus` is a focus event. It does not move the mouse.

Folding the reachable sequence against the shipped branch set:

| # | Interaction | focused | hovered | pointerAcquired | cue drawn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | hover enter | F | **T** | F | no |
| 2 | press | F | T | **T** | no |
| 3 | focus | **T** | T | T | no — correct, mouse-acquired |
| 4 | unfocus | F | **F ← the defect** | F | no |
| 5 | focus | **T** | F | F | yes — correct so far, this looks like a fresh Tab |
| 6 | press | T | F | **stays F** | **yes ← a mouse press draws the focus ring** |

Step 4 is the only wrong cell. The pointer never moved, so no hover-exit was emitted and no hover-enter can ever be re-emitted; `hovered` is stuck false while the pointer is physically resting on the control. Step 6's press guard reads that stale false and declines to mark the focus as pointer-acquired.

Preserving the one pointer-derived field across the focus reset makes step 4 leave `hovered = T`, so step 6's guard fires, `pointerAcquired` becomes true and the cue stays suppressed — while step 5 still yields a visible cue for the genuine Tab case (that case reaches step 5 with `hovered` false because a real hover-exit was emitted, which is exactly what `recoveryUnfocusThenFreshFocusWithNoHoverIsVisibleAgain` already asserts and must keep asserting).

The existing test stops after step 5. That is why this shipped: the suite proves the fix does not break Tab, and never once presses the button afterwards.

**Why a composed test as well as the fold test.** The fold test constructs `Interaction` objects by hand and is the precise, deterministic statement of the reducer contract. It cannot tell you whether the real `AeroSwitch` modifier chain actually produces that sequence — whether a focus move really emits an unfocus without a hover-exit, whether the press really lands on the same source. That reachability claim is the load-bearing half of CR-02 (a defect that cannot be reached is not a blocker), and the project's own false-positive-sign-off lesson is precisely that a green suite proving a mechanism is not a proof that the path is exercised. One composed test closes that gap for one component; the reducer being shared carries it to the other three.
</planner_finding>
